# Initial QA Practitioner Conversation Agenda

Use this agenda for the first listening conversation with a QA practitioner who does not report directly to the QA & Test Engineering Manager. It is a chance to understand their experience of delivery, quality practice, development interests, and the support they need across the function.

This is not a performance review, an objective-setting meeting, or a substitute for their line manager's development conversations. Do not make commitments about objectives, progression, or team moves without coordinating with their line manager. After the call, keep only durable, work-relevant findings in the relevant team profile, QA health summary, discovery note, challenge, decision, or goal.

## Conversation Aim

Understand how the person experiences their role and delivery environment, what enables or obstructs effective QA work, where they want to develop, and how QA leadership can make a practical difference. Keep the discussion curious and collaborative rather than evaluative.

## 1. Open The Conversation

- Explain that this is an initial listening conversation, not a formal performance or goal-setting meeting.
- Say that you want an honest view of what is working, what is difficult, and where you can be useful across the QA function.
- Confirm that their line manager remains responsible for their day-to-day development and formal objectives.
- Explain that you will look for recurring themes across conversations without unnecessarily attributing individual comments.

## 2. How They Work

- How do you like to work day to day, and what does a good delivery process look like for you?
- How does QA fit into refinement, delivery, release, and follow-up in your team?
- Where does work flow well, and where does it queue, stall, or create avoidable rework?
- How early can you influence requirements, acceptance criteria, testability, and quality risk?
- How do you work with developers, product, delivery, and other QAs when a change is unclear or risky?

## 3. Role, Team, And Effectiveness

- How are you feeling about the role, your team, and QA more generally?
- What parts of the work give you energy or feel most valuable?
- What takes longer than it should, interrupts focus, or causes recurring frustration?
- Where do environments, test data, builds, deployments, access, or dependencies get in the way?
- What makes it hard to test from the customer's perspective?
- Which quality activities feel like meaningful engineering work, and which feel unnecessarily manual or reactive?

## 4. Quality Practice And Collaboration

- Which automation, tooling, or quality signals do you trust, and which do you treat cautiously?
- Where are the most important coverage gaps or recurring delivery risks?
- How visible are test outcomes and quality risks to the people who need to act on them?
- Where could better communication, shared knowledge, or earlier developer involvement improve delivery confidence?
- What knowledge do you have that would be useful to share more widely, and what knowledge would help you work more effectively?

## 5. Development And Support

- Where would you like to develop over the next 12-18 months?
- What work would give you useful stretch, variety, or greater ownership?
- What support from your line manager, the QA leadership team, or the wider function would make the biggest difference?
- Are there opportunities to collaborate across teams or platforms that would help your development and the wider practice?
- What should I understand before deciding where to focus QA-wide support?

## 6. Web Team 2 Nuance: KB

Use these prompts for KB's initial conversation. They are optional; keep the call natural rather than treating them as a checklist.

- How is Web Team 2 operating at the moment, and how consistently is QA involved from feature shaping through to release?
- How confident are you in the web automation and delivery signals available to the team?
- Where do preview environments, backend dependencies, test data, market variation, or CI feedback make testing harder than it should be?
- How are complex or cross-cutting features planned and tested, especially where the customer journey spans multiple services?
- How does the team use your CEP and automation experience, and where could that expertise have more impact?
- What would make collaboration with Web Teams 1 and 3, Mobile, or Payments more useful?
- As a senior QA contributor, what opportunities would help you broaden technical influence or develop toward your preferred next step?

### Relevant Context

- KB is a Senior QA Engineer II (Level 6) in Website Team 2 and reports to DA.
- KB has web automation, CEP, and complex feature-testing experience.
- Website quality work spans team boundaries and can be affected by preview environments, backend dependencies, test data, CI feedback, and market variation.
- Keep any development observations distinct from DA's formal management and progression responsibilities.

## 7. Website QA: JFG

Use these prompts for JFG's initial conversation. They are optional; keep the call natural rather than treating them as a checklist.

## 8. Website QA: JS

Use these prompts for JS's initial conversation. They are optional; keep the call natural rather than treating them as a checklist.

- How is QA involvement working in your current Website team, from early feature discussion through to release?
- Where are accessibility considerations built into delivery, and where do they arrive too late or become difficult to validate?
- How useful and maintainable is the `pico.frontend` Playwright pack in practice, and what would improve its value to the team?
- How do front-end changes, shared components, and commercial or market variation affect the test approach?
- Which parts of your functional, accessibility, and automation experience would you like more opportunity to use or develop?
- What knowledge from previous front-end teams or COP work could help the wider QA practice?
- What support from QA leadership would make the biggest practical difference to your work and development?

### Relevant Context

- JS is a QA Engineer (Level 3/4, exact level to confirm) embedded in Website delivery and reports to DA.
- JS has web functional-testing and accessibility experience, has worked in several front-end teams and COP, and coauthored the `pico.frontend` Playwright automation pack.
- Explore how accessibility and automation are being integrated into ordinary delivery work, rather than assuming they need a separate process.
- Keep any development observations distinct from DA's formal management and progression responsibilities.

## 9. Close

- What is the one thing you would most like me to understand from this conversation?
- Is there anything you expected me to ask but have not?
- Who else should I speak to, or which forum would help me understand this better?
- What would make the next few weeks feel useful rather than disruptive?

## After The Call

- Capture themes, not a transcript.
- Record operational actions in Notion, Jira, or Teams.
- Add only durable context to the person's [team profile](../team/README.md), [QA Health Summary](../context/qa-health-summary.md), or a discovery note.
- Coordinate any material development, workload, progression, or team-movement finding with the person's line manager.
