Role Purpose 

To be the most senior QA engineer within a platform area, setting the test engineering ceiling, owning the hardest automation and quality architecture problems, and building the things that make your teammates significantly faster and more confident in what they ship. You operate at the intersection of deep test engineering craft and cross-team quality influence: embedded in your platform, connected to the wider QA function through the chapter and a close working relationship with the QA and Test Engineering Manager, and consistently raising both the quality of the work and the capability of the people around you. 

About the Team 

The QA and Test Engineering team is part of PureGym’s Software Engineering function, with a clear purpose: ensuring the quality, reliability, and correctness of the web and app products that acquire and retain members at every PureGym. We’re hyper-focused on five key business outcomes: converting visitors to members, retaining and winning back subscribers, expanding rapidly into new markets, driving acquisition traffic, and supporting PureGym’s low-cost, self-service model. 

The team sits alongside Software Engineering and Software Delivery, with QA professionals matrixed into our delivery squads. The team is organised by technology platform: website, payments, and mobile app. The team operates in a high-engineering-velocity environment, which means automation is required as the foundation of how we maintain quality at pace. We’re AI-first and expect our most senior QA engineers to be at the forefront of what agentic testing, AI-assisted exploratory testing, and intelligent test automation make possible. 

The Role 

PureGym’s digital platforms power millions of member interactions and are evolving at pace. As Staff QA Analyst, you are the test engineering anchor of your platform area. You own the hardest automation and quality architecture decisions, set the quality bar that others reach toward, and take responsibility for the technical health of everything your platform tests across. Your influence does not stop at your squad boundary: through the QA chapter and cross-team collaboration, you connect your platform’s quality direction to the broader function and contribute to raising standards across it. 

You lead through craft and judgment as much as through direction: the best measure of your impact is not the quality of your own test suites alone, it is the ceiling you set for others and the multiplier things you build that make the QA engineers and software engineers around you faster, safer, and more confident. At Staff level, your impact extends beyond what you build: you may carry line management responsibility for a small number of QA practitioners, and the growth of those people is as much a measure of your success as the quality of the automation estate you own. 

You work in close partnership with the QA and Test Engineering Manager, who drives quality strategy and standards across the whole function. Where the Manager works across all platforms, you are the deepest quality contributor in yours. You bring that broader direction to life in the work your platform (or platforms) actually ships, and you surface ground-level reality back to the Manager to keep their strategic judgment honest. 

You’ll be responsible for: 

- Setting the test engineering direction for your platform area: leading automation architecture decisions and ensuring the test estate is moving in the right direction with each iteration

- Contributing to the design and reliability of the test automation infrastructure that underpins quality at scale: harness services that provision test accounts and seed test data consistently, environment parity across dev, staging, and production-equivalent environments, and automation that runs dependably without manual intervention or fragile setup; surfacing platform-level infrastructure needs and helping shape solutions that work across the function

- Building with a multiplier mindset: identifying opportunities to create shared test utilities, reusable automation components, and test infrastructure that reduces duplication across the function and raises the confidence of the teams that rely on them

- Producing the function’s highest-quality test and code reviews: thorough, direct, and educational, consistently raising the bar rather than just approving work

- Providing line management for a small number of QA practitioners: regular 1:1s, development conversations, honest performance feedback, enforcement of policies and standards, and support for their growth and progression through the growth framework

- Mentoring Lead QA Analysts and senior QA engineers, investing in their technical growth through pairing, challenge, and honest feedback on the work rather than the person; this is one of your most important contributions, and it is treated as real work, not extra-curricular

- Contributing to upstream initiative shaping alongside the QA and Test Engineering Manager: stress-testing proposed solutions from a quality and testability perspective, surfacing risk during Discovery, and ensuring work arrives at the initiative acceptance point with acceptance criteria, edge cases, and test approach already understood

- Acting as the platform’s senior quality voice when the squad accepts an initiative: assessing whether it is testable as scoped, whether acceptance criteria are sufficient, and whether the quality approach is achievable within the agreed delivery window; raising concerns before the squad commits rather than after; and once accepted, owning the quality continuity from acceptance through to delivery, hypercare, and close

- Connecting your platform’s quality decisions to the wider QA function through active chapter participation and collaboration with Lead QA Analysts and Staff QA Engineers across other platforms

- Leading AI-accelerated test engineering within your platform: thinking and working agentic-first, directing agents across test generation, exploratory execution, regression analysis, and coverage assessment; building repeatable agentic test workflows and coaching the team to do the same; measuring delivery in outcomes and confidence shipped, not test cases written

- Actively managing test debt across your platform’s automation estate: surfacing it clearly, making the case for addressing it, and driving incremental resolution rather than letting it accumulate quietly

- Setting the standard for shared quality ownership: ensuring software engineers treat testability, test automation, and acceptance criteria as their responsibility alongside QA, not something to hand over; partnering with engineers as delivery collaborators and helping the squad build the discipline where quality is built in from the start, not verified at the end

- Holding DORA quality outcomes (change failure rate and mean time to restore) as first-class quality metrics and making them visible within your platform; using them as signals of engineering health and leading the response when they deteriorate

- Setting the platform’s flow discipline for quality work: ensuring test work does not become a bottleneck at the end of the sprint; stepping into whatever is needed to keep flow moving; treating WIP accumulation as a systemic signal and reinforcing the norm that done means deployed and verified

- Holding NFR test coverage within your platform: accessibility, performance, security, and uptime, and escalating clearly when delivery pressure is creating risk to standards the function holds itself to

- Acting as an external technical voice for PureGym’s quality engineering practice at industry events, technical communities, and engineering forums, building brand presence and bringing external learning back into the function

Technology Stack 

Our teams build and run PureGym’s digital estate using a modern, event-driven technology landscape: a fast Astro-powered website, a .NET MAUI native app, event-driven .NET microservices, Payload CMS, and integrations with Braze, Exerp, and our strategic payment partners. It’s a genuinely exciting stack, built for pace, scale, and quality, and the QA function tests across all of it. 

How We Work 

We deliver software through a structured initiative lifecycle (Intake, In Discovery, Ready for Development, In Development, Hypercare and Measurement, and Closed) designed to reduce context-switching, minimise cross-team dependencies, and keep work visible from idea to close. Initiatives are tightly scoped to a short, focused delivery window; anything larger must be sliced before a team takes it on. The delivery window is agreed during Discovery and held firm: scope that won’t fit it doesn’t get accepted when the squad commits. 

Within that lifecycle, squads operate on a two-week sprint cadence using vanilla Scrum or Kanban depending on the nature of their work. Our tooling is practical and widely adopted: Jira for planning and tracking, Confluence for documentation, Microsoft Teams for collaboration, GitHub for source control, and Azure as our cloud platform. Miro when we need to think visually. 

AI is central to how we work, not an add-on. The QA function is in a particularly exciting position here: agentic test generation, AI-assisted exploratory testing, and intelligent defect and coverage analysis are real, fast-moving capabilities. The Staff QA Analyst is expected to be ahead of the team on this and to set the pace of adoption. 

The Person 

You are a deep test engineering expert who has stayed grounded. You have the automation range to see across a platform’s quality needs and the practical instincts to know which problems actually matter. You have operated at a senior QA level long enough to know that influence at this level is earned through what you build, what you teach, and how reliably your quality judgment turns out to be right. 

You have a multiplier mindset. You are not satisfied by the quality of your own test suites. You measure yourself by the ceiling you set for others and the things you build that make your team significantly faster and more confident. When you spot a testing pattern that three teams are solving independently, you help align them to create the shared solution. When a Lead QA is tackling an automation architecture problem for the first time, you give them the thinking tools rather than the answer. 

You are genuinely excited by what AI makes possible in quality engineering, not theoretically but practically. You have already moved past using AI as a test generation shortcut and are thinking about how to orchestrate agents across full testing cycles. You model agentic-first approaches for your platform and bring the rest of the team on that journey with you. 

You are comfortable holding both the technical and the people leadership conversation in the same week. You can spend the morning working through an automation architecture problem and the afternoon in a development conversation with one of your direct reports. The job requires both, and you bring energy to both. 

You are comfortable being embedded in a platform while reaching beyond it. Your accountability is your platform’s quality and the growth of the people in your team, and your influence extends across the function through the chapter, cross-team collaboration, and your partnership with the QA and Test Engineering Manager. 

You hold the line on quality without being a gate. You surface test debt and NFR risk clearly, make the business case for addressing them, and do not let delivery pressure erode the standards the function holds itself to.
