# Competency: People Management

## Scale

3 levels: Learn, Execute, Mentor

### Score 1 - Learn
- Description: Supports the development of less experienced colleagues through practical coaching and grounded feedback, without needing formal line responsibility. Turns broad growth goals into clear next steps and shows reliable readiness to support others' progression.
- Expectation: Supports onboarding and growth with guidance; gives timely, constructive feedback.
- Expected example: A software engineer works with a less experienced colleague on a clear improvement focus, for example code review quality, and checks progress over several sprints rather than a one-off comment. A QA engineer gives a junior colleague specific feedback on test design that helps them understand both the gap and the next step. Both support a development goal with practical coaching, pairing, or examples rather than leaving it theoretical.
- Stretch example: Takes a leading role in helping one or more colleagues make visible progression over a quarter or two. Supports a tougher development reset with calm, direct, useful feedback rather than avoiding the conversation. Materially improves the quality of a development conversation led by the person's line manager through the input they provide.

### Score 2 - Execute
- Description: Acts like an effective people leader even without the formal reporting line, setting clear expectations and addressing gaps directly, balancing recognition with accountability so people grow in both capability and confidence.
- Expectation: Manages performance and development for a small team with effective 1:1s and clear goals.
- Expected example: A software engineer holds repeated coaching conversations that lead to visible improvement in a teammate's code quality or delivery pace. A QA engineer helps a colleague understand testing expectations clearly and follows up when their coverage quality stalls rather than letting it drift. Both use recognition and challenge well, so people feel supported without the bar dropping.
- Stretch example: Handles a difficult performance or behaviour concern constructively and helps move it toward resolution with the person's line manager. Builds more autonomy in several colleagues by coaching them to solve more of their own problems. Becomes a trusted development guide for a wider group, not just the people closest to them.

### Score 3 - Mentor
- Description: Strengthens the wider people environment by developing other senior colleagues in how they support growth well, providing trusted input into progression and succession conversations beyond one-to-one support.
- Expectation: Builds high-performing teams, handles difficult conversations, and grows talent intentionally.
- Expected example: A software engineer develops less experienced colleagues in ways that clearly raise their confidence and impact, and helps create a coaching pattern others reuse consistently. A QA engineer provides trusted input into a colleague's promotion case based on genuine, tracked development over time. Both help create better progression or coaching patterns that raise the standard for the group, not just the individual they are mentoring.
- Stretch example: Shapes a stronger development culture across the squad so more people grow well without constant manager intervention. Creates or improves a development approach that materially strengthens progression readiness for future leaders. Becomes a genuine multiplier for coaching and succession by helping other people become better developers of talent themselves.
