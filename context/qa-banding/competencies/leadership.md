# Competency: Leadership

## Scale

4 levels: Learn, Execute, Mentor, Manage

### Score 1 - Learn
- Description: Shows leadership in early but meaningful ways, helping maintain psychological safety, supporting teammates without taking over, and keeping conversations anchored on learning and outcomes rather than blame. Begins to influence the squad's tone through calm behaviour and follow-through, at a pace appropriate to someone new to the team.
- Expectation: Demonstrates ownership, supports peers, and contributes to a psychologically safe team environment.
- Expected example: A software engineer helps make it safe for a teammate to ask a question in front of the whole squad rather than stay stuck silently. A QA engineer encourages the team to reflect honestly after a missed defect rather than letting it pass unexamined, helping the squad actually learn from it. Both reinforce the squad's agreed norms when work gets rushed or tense.
- Stretch example: Facilitates a difficult discussion so the group leaves with clarity and a better tone, not just more noise. Supports a teammate through a confidence dip after a mistake and helps them recover constructively rather than disengage. Improves a small team habit around reflection or feedback so it becomes more consistent.

### Score 2 - Execute
- Description: Demonstrates the squad's ways of working clearly and helps others align to them, using feedback and reflective practice, retrospectives, incident reviews, to improve standards over time. Notices when someone is struggling or disengaged and steps in constructively rather than waiting for it to become a bigger problem.
- Expectation: Leads small initiatives, facilitates retrospectives, and models team values and ways of working.
- Expected example: A software engineer leads a retrospective that produces clear actions with visible follow-through in the next sprint, not just a list that gets forgotten. A QA engineer resets the squad's tone when testing standards start slipping under delivery pressure, and the change failure rate improves as a result. Both help a struggling teammate handle a difficult moment with more ownership and self-awareness.
- Stretch example: Creates a stronger leadership environment across the squad by helping several teammates grow in confidence and accountability at once. Turns a recurring behavioural or process issue into a practical improvement pattern the squad actually sustains. Starts to hold a clear picture of what strong squad performance looks like and helps others align to it.

### Score 3 - Mentor
- Description: Creates space for others to grow, speak plainly, and learn quickly, guiding difficult discussions to clear outcomes and encouraging calculated risk over blame. Builds confidence and capability in other emerging leaders rather than being the only steady voice in the squad.
- Expectation: Leads through influence within their team, mentors others, and coaches for growth and accountability; contributes to culture across teams through chapters and shared practices.
- Expected example: A software engineer guides a difficult technical disagreement to a clear decision without losing trust or momentum for the sprint. A QA engineer supports less experienced colleagues through honest, direct feedback that visibly raises their confidence and the standard of their test coverage. Both shape the squad's tone so people can speak plainly and stay accountable to each other under pace.
- Stretch example: Builds a stronger leadership culture across the squad, not just within individual relationships, so more people step up without being asked. Creates space for a future leader to step forward rather than centralising influence in themselves. Influences behaviour or standards beyond the squad through chapters or cross-team leadership moments.

### Score 4 - Manage
- Description: Leadership is earned through craft and multiplier impact rather than authority: it sets the ceiling other Software and QA engineers reach toward, works in partnership with the Principal and QA and Test Engineering Manager to connect squad-level culture to the wider function. Strengthens leadership culture beyond individual relationships, creating the conditions for other engineers, QA practitioners, and future leaders to step forward, and improves practices through chapters and communities to raise trust and long-term effectiveness, not just the day's delivery.
- Expectation: Earns leadership through craft and multiplier impact, sets the standard others aspire to, develops future leaders, and connects squad culture to the wider function through chapters and partnership with the Principal Software Engineer or QA and Test Engineering Manager.
- Expected example: Develops other senior colleagues in ways that measurably raise their confidence and technical judgment, mentoring not with answers but with thinking tools so the squad's technical ceiling rises over time. Strengthens a chapter or community of practice, developing the people in it so leadership and standards rise across platforms, not just within their own squad. Creates room for other leaders to step forward instead of holding all the influence themselves, and connects their platform's standards to the wider function through active chapter leadership.
- Stretch example: Builds a local succession pipeline strong enough that the squad's technical or quality ceiling keeps rising even when they step back. Shapes a broader leadership or capability improvement with chapter or function leaders that other squads or platforms later adopt, grounded in what actually worked rather than theory. Represents PureGym engineering or QA externally in a way that reinforces the standard the whole function holds itself to.
