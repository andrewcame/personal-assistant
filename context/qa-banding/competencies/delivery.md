# Competency: Delivery

## Scale

5 levels: Learn, Execute, Mentor, Manage, Strategise

### Score 1 - Learn
- Description: Completes assigned work properly and builds delivery habits that contribute to predictable flow and timely completion of work. Understands what done means, deployed and verified, not just code-complete, and treats documentation and verification as part of the job rather than something to skip when time is short.
- Expectation: Delivers small tasks with guidance, follows team processes, and documents work.
- Expected example: A software engineer completes a small task fully, with acceptance criteria met and notes updated, so it can be picked up, reviewed, and closed without confusion. A QA engineer executes a defined set of tests reliably and flags a blocker the moment it appears rather than sitting on it until stand-up. Both start work with a clear understanding of what needs to be true for it to count as finished.
- Stretch example: Owns a small, well-defined piece of work end-to-end with minimal prompting and gets it safely to done inside the sprint. Anticipates a simple dependency or verification need early enough to avoid delay later in the task. Improves a small checklist or note that makes the next similar task faster for whoever picks it up next.

### Score 2 - Execute
- Description: Independently drives well-defined work to verified completion, sequencing steps sensibly and treating delivery as ownership of the whole path, ticket to production, not just the build. Keeps pace predictable by pulling work to done before starting something new, in a delivery model where scope that does not fit the agreed window does not get accepted.
- Expectation: Delivers well-defined work independently, prioritises effectively, and maintains quality and predictability.
- Expected example: A software engineer owns a story from pickup to production and verification without losing control of the details, breaking a moderately complex change into slices that move through build, test, and release without avoidable churn. A QA engineer plans and executes coverage that keeps pace with the squad's sprint cadence, prioritising completion over starting new test work so nothing queues up at the end of the sprint. Both treat done as deployed and verified.
- Stretch example: Plans a moderately complex piece of work across several stories in a way that keeps delivery incremental and safe rather than one large risky release. Spots a delivery or test risk early enough to reshape the plan before it causes slippage against the agreed delivery window. Helps turn a loosely formed task into something the squad can commit to with confidence.

### Score 3 - Mentor
- Description: Improves delivery beyond their own tasks, breaking complex work into incremental, shippable outcomes, and raising the standard of task-shaping and completion discipline for the people around them by helping your team get work over the line. Balances planned and unplanned work well, so the squad's pace stays predictable even when priorities shift.
- Expectation: Leads delivery of features, improves flow and automation, and mentors others on execution.
- Expected example: A software engineer breaks a complex feature into deliverables that ship safely and let the squad learn as they go, rather than one big-bang release. A QA engineer holds change failure rate and mean time to restore as first-class metrics in the squad and contributes directly to the response when either slips. Both help a less experienced colleague get work fully over the line, including rollout and verification, rather than leaving it mostly done.
- Stretch example: Leads delivery of a small initiative or epic slice from planning through production, keeping it moving without stalling. Resets a piece of work that is getting bloated or stuck by simplifying scope and restoring a realistic path to done inside the delivery window. Introduces a delivery pattern that measurably reduces partial work or repeated re-planning for the squad.

### Score 4 - Manage
- Description: Leads initiatives through shaping, sequencing, and follow-through, surfacing delivery risk early enough to act on it. Uses documentation, DORA signals, and delivery habits as active tools for predictability, and models the discipline that a feature in production is always worth more than two in progress.
- Expectation: Owns delivery for a team or domain; manages dependencies, metrics, and trade-offs with tech debt.
- Expected example: Leads or shares ownership of the technical thread from initiative acceptance through delivery and hypercare, keeping the squad's commitment realistic and its pace sustainable. Sets the platform's flow discipline for test work so it never becomes the bottleneck at the end of a sprint, reinforcing that done means deployed and verified. Re-sequences work when reality changes without letting work-in-progress sprawl.
- Stretch example: Steps in early on an over-scoped initiative and reshapes it into something the squad can commit to and actually deliver within its window. Creates a delivery or quality guardrail that measurably improves flow or completion discipline beyond their own squad. Leads incident follow-through in a way that strengthens the squad's delivery habits afterward rather than just closing the ticket.

### Score 5 - Strategise
- Description: Brings the highest level of delivery judgment inside the squad, calibrating complex or high-risk initiatives before commitment and coaching others on slicing, sequencing, and deliverability. Feeds what works back into wider engineering or QA practice through chapters and partnership with the Principal Software Engineer or QA and Test Engineering Manager.
- Expectation: Drives portfolio delivery across teams, aligns to strategy, and optimises flow at scale.
- Expected example: Provides senior calibration on a complex initiative before commitment, so the squad accepts work it can genuinely deliver inside the agreed window rather than discovering the problem mid-sprint. Partners on upstream initiative shaping, stress-testing solutions from a quality and testability angle so the squad enters Discovery with fewer surprises. Turns a useful delivery pattern into guidance others reuse through chapters.
- Stretch example: Partners on a delivery or shaping standard that several squads later adopt, visible in fewer missed delivery windows across the function. Resets a high-risk initiative early enough inside the squad to avoid a costly delivery miss that would otherwise hit members or a market launch. Uses evidence from squad delivery outcomes, DORA metrics, escaped defects, delivery predictability, to influence a wider improvement in delivery discipline.
